1. Product vision

Aurelia is a project-centric creative workspace for fiction authors. It brings manuscript organization, interconnected notes, structured worldbuilding, and practical writing tools into one calm environment.

It takes useful ideas from writing and knowledge-management tools—manuscript organization, linked notes, structured records, and rich editing—without becoming a generic note-taking app or an AI text generator.

Core differentiator: an author can develop an entire fictional universe and write multiple books within it, with characters, locations, events, research, and lore shared and linked rather than duplicated.

2. Product principles

Author owns their work

Projects are local files first, readable and portable. Cloud services are optional.

Structure without restriction

Offer useful hierarchy and templates without forcing every author into one plotting method.

Everything is connected

Characters, places, scenes, events, and notes link together and remain linked when renamed or moved.

Writing comes first

The editor remains the centre of the experience. Panels and tools support rather than crowd the manuscript.

Intelligence without dependency

AI is optional. Core writing, organization, search, and worldbuilding work offline without AI.

Calm, dependable software

Protect work through predictable saving, undo, snapshots, conflict handling, and clear status.

3. Workspace and project structure

The foundational model should be project-centric, not document-centric. A workspace can contain several fictional universes and standalone books. A universe can contain multiple books that share worldbuilding and research.

Aurelia Workspace
├── Universe: Example Universe
│   ├── Book 1
│   │   └── Arcs → Chapters → Scenes
│   ├── Book 2
│   ├── Book 3
│   ├── Shared Characters
│   ├── Shared Worlds & Locations
│   ├── Shared Lore & Research
│   └── Shared Timeline & Events
├── Universe: Another Universe
└── Standalone Books

Core entities

  • Workspace: top-level container for a user's creative projects.
  • Universe: shared setting, canon, history, characters, and reference material.
  • Book: manuscript and its book-specific planning structure.
  • Arc: narrative thread, theme, or character/story progression.
  • Chapter and Scene: ordered manuscript units with objectives and metadata.
  • Character, World, Place, Item, Event, Note, Research: structured records that can be shared and linked.

Standalone books may be represented as books without a universe association, or through an implicit private universe. Choose one model consistently before implementation.

Architecture constraint: universe ownership and book-specific ownership must be explicit from the beginning. This affects storage, permissions, linking, indexing, and sync.

4. Writing experience

Primary layout

  • Top bar: workspace/project switcher, navigation, search, command palette, sync and account/settings access.
  • Left navigation: workspace, universes, books, manuscript outline, and reference libraries.
  • Centre: manuscript editor, receiving the majority of screen space.
  • Right contextual panel: scene/chapter/arc objectives, metadata, linked entities, and backlinks.
  • Bottom status bar: live word counts, location in manuscript, save state, and sync state.

Editor requirements

  • Structured rich-text editor with reliable transaction-based undo/redo—not a plain textarea.
  • Markdown-style typing with immediate rich rendering (WYSIWYM).
  • Visual toolbar for users who do not know Markdown.
  • Toggle between rendered editing and Markdown source without losing content.
  • Headings, paragraphs, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, lists, checklists, blockquotes, tables, callouts, scene breaks, footnotes (optional), internal links, and entity embeds.
  • Defined Markdown dialect and tested import/export round-tripping. Do not promise support for every Markdown extension without tests.
  • Stable block identifiers where needed for comments, annotations, and reliable references.
  • Distraction-free mode; configurable editor font, size, line height, and paragraph spacing.
  • Dockable, collapsible panels with saved layout preferences.

Contextual writing panels

ContextUseful fields and tools
ArcTheme, narrative purpose, objective, status, related characters, books/scenes affected.
ChapterChapter objective, summary, target word count, checklist, status, associated arcs.
ScenePOV, location, time, objective, conflict, outcome, status, word target, characters present, arcs advanced.

Panels should be configurable. The editor remains the primary focus, not a dashboard full of mandatory fields.

Navigation and productivity

  • Command palette (for example, Ctrl/Cmd+K), keyboard navigation, recent and pinned items.
  • Global search across titles, content, tags, and entity types.
  • Outliner with drag-and-drop reordering.
  • Corkboard/storyboard for visual scene planning.
  • Timeline view and configurable columns for POV, status, character, or plot thread.

5. Worldbuilding and reference library

Character sheets

  • Identity, role, status, appearance, biography, background, and portrait/reference image gallery.
  • Motivations, goals, fears, values, flaws, skills, secrets, and voice/dialogue style.
  • Relationships with typed links (family, ally, rival, mentor, etc.) and descriptions.
  • Character development and appearances across scenes, chapters, and books.
  • Tags, notes, timeline events, and links to places, items, and story arcs.

Worlds, planets, and places

  • Create fictional planets or use Earth as a world root.
  • Planet profile: physical properties, orbit/star, gravity, climate, moons, day/year length, calendars, and geography.
  • Flexible place hierarchy: planet → continent → country → region → city → building → room, with custom levels allowed.
  • Place profiles: sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste), atmosphere, population, culture, dangers, history, map/coordinates, and images.
  • Worldbuilding references: cultures, factions, beliefs, languages, technology or magic systems, economy, history, and events.
  • Interactive maps and visual geography tools can be introduced after the core data model is stable.

Items, artifacts, events, and lore

  • Items/artifacts: description, origin, provenance, properties, significance, restrictions, and linked scenes/characters/places.
  • Timeline events: date or date range, participants, locations, consequences, and links to manuscript scenes.
  • Research and lore records with source references, notes, tags, and links.

6. Knowledge, links, and discovery

  • Every entity has a permanent stable ID independent of its title or file path.
  • Support internal links such as [[Character Name]], with autocomplete and fuzzy matching.
  • Support explicit stable-ID links such as [[id:stable-id]] for unambiguous references.
  • Renaming or moving an entity must not break links.
  • Every entity has “Links to” and “Linked from”/backlinks views.
  • Search across entity title, body, tags, type, and metadata.
  • Graph view for relationships among characters, places, scenes, events, arcs, and lore.
  • Embed linked entity cards or summaries in manuscript and notes.

Keep user-facing syntax convenient while resolving links internally through stable IDs. Handle duplicate names with an entity picker rather than silently choosing the wrong record.

7. Local storage, portability, and sync

Local-first project files

Basic writing and worldbuilding must work without an account, cloud backend, or network connection. Use readable project files with structured metadata and a local index.

/AureliaWorkspace/
  workspace.json
  /universes/
    /universe-id/
      universe.md
      /books/
        /book-id/
          book.json
          /manuscript/
            /chapters/
              chapter-id.md
      /characters/
      /worlds/
      /places/
      /items/
      /events/
      /research/
      /media/
  /standalone-books/
  /.aurelia/
    index.db
    device-state.json
    sync-manifest.json

This is an illustrative layout, not a locked schema. Use Markdown with YAML frontmatter for human-readable content where suitable, and JSON for structured metadata where it improves validation and evolution. Keep generated indexes and caches separate from canonical user content.

Browser storage and portability

  • Use a storage abstraction rather than binding the entire app to one browser API.
  • Use the File System Access API where supported, with clear permission/reconnection UX.
  • Provide a fallback for browsers/platforms without equivalent folder access, using supported local storage such as OPFS/IndexedDB plus import/export.
  • Handle permission loss, quota limits, interrupted writes, file renames, and recovery explicitly.
  • Use safe-write patterns and recovery journals where appropriate; do not assume all browser/filesystem combinations provide identical atomic-write guarantees.

Bring-your-own-cloud approach

“Any cloud provider” is best approached through interoperable storage paths and protocols rather than promising a direct OAuth integration with every provider.

MethodApproach
Synced local folderUser places the Aurelia workspace inside a provider's desktop sync folder (OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive for desktop, iCloud Drive, Syncthing, NAS, etc.).
WebDAVConnect to compatible storage endpoints with user-configured credentials.
S3-compatibleSupport custom endpoint and bucket configuration for compatible object storage, such as S3-compatible services and self-hosted options.
Native provider connectorsConsider Google Drive, OneDrive, and Dropbox OAuth integrations later, based on demand and maintenance cost.

Sync safety requirements

  • Use content hashes and manifests; do not rely on timestamps alone.
  • Track per-device state and remote revisions/ETags where available.
  • Use optimistic concurrency and detect divergent edits.
  • Never silently overwrite a conflict. Preserve both versions and provide a conflict-resolution workflow.
  • Do not assume file locking works consistently across sync providers.
  • Make sync status and errors visible, with retry and recovery options.

Optional password-protected sync

  • Offer client-side encryption before data leaves the device; design for a zero-knowledge service model.
  • Encrypt content and sensitive metadata/manifests if claiming full workspace privacy.
  • Use a vetted password KDF (for example, Argon2id) and authenticated encryption from established libraries; never invent cryptography.
  • Never store the user's sync password. Explain that losing it may make encrypted data unrecoverable.
  • Keep credentials and secrets out of project files, logs, source control, and telemetry.

8. Optional AI capabilities

AI is a modular enhancement, not a requirement for using Aurelia. It should be disabled until the user configures a provider and explicitly chooses what context to share.

Potential capabilities

  • Tone, voice, pacing, and style analysis.
  • Character consistency checks across scenes where a character appears.
  • Arc progression analysis against author-defined objectives.
  • Scene/chapter summaries and narrative progress reviews.
  • Research Q&A with source citations and a record of references.
  • Revision suggestions, continuity checks, and fact-check task lists.
  • Optional prompt manager and reusable analysis templates.

Privacy and control

  • Scope context to selected text, current scene, chapter, book, universe, or entire workspace.
  • Require explicit permission before sending broader context.
  • Show what is being sent, to which provider, and for what purpose.
  • Return suggestions as comments or proposed changes; never silently rewrite the manuscript.
  • Use a provider adapter so supported cloud or local model providers can be changed without rewriting the app.
Account and billing distinction: a ChatGPT subscription is not automatically equivalent to API access for a separate third-party app. Typical approaches are a developer-managed API integration or a user-supplied API key. Verify current provider authentication and product terms before implementing any account connection.

9. Non-AI writing tools

  • Offline spellcheck and grammar assistance, independent of AI.
  • Custom project dictionary for character names, invented words, places, and terminology.
  • Repeated-word detection, long-sentence indicators, and optional style heuristics.
  • Inline underlines, a suggestion panel, ignore/add-to-dictionary actions, and clear explanations.
  • Grammar engine should be configurable and should not block typing or saving.

Default preference: grammar/spell checking should be OFF by default, reflecting the initial product requirement that it normally be checked off. Users can enable it globally or per project. Distinguish spelling errors, grammar suggestions, and heuristic style signals; do not present uncertain heuristics as definitive corrections.

10. Import, export, history, and later collaboration

Import

  • Markdown folders and supported plain-text formats should be first-class import paths.
  • Build a robust import pipeline with preview, mapping, validation, and a report of unsupported content.
  • Investigate Scrivener, Ulysses, Campfire, and World Anvil import paths. Support only validated project/file variants; do not promise universal compatibility.

Export

  • Markdown project bundle for portability and version control.
  • DOCX, PDF, and EPUB manuscript export.
  • Customizable front matter, chapter breaks, typography, and export profiles.
  • MOBI is low priority and should not be a launch requirement.

History and collaboration

  • Version snapshots, compare/diff, and restore.
  • Optional beta-reader sharing, comments, and revision mode as a later phase.
  • Use a sound transaction/document model, but do not imply real-time multi-user collaboration is included merely because the editor supports transactions.
  • Defer real-time collaboration until the single-user local-first experience and sync model are reliable.

11. Visual identity and design system

Recommended direction: elegant, scholarly, calm, intelligent, and timeless. A restrained black-and-gold identity fits the working name and desired atmosphere.

Core palette

#0B0B0F Aurelia Black
#F5C542 Aurelia Gold
#FAF7F2 Warm Parchment
#2A2A33 Slate
#C9A227 Muted Gold
#14141A Dark Panel
#EDEDED Light Text

Typography

UI: Inter or Source Sans 3
Editor: Literata or Crimson Pro
Code: JetBrains Mono
Self-host fonts where practical and verify font licenses.

Theme guidance

  • Light mode: parchment background, near-black text, restrained gold accents.
  • Dark mode: near-black background, dark panels, light text, restrained gold accents.
  • Use darker gold/brown for small text on light backgrounds where needed for contrast; reserve brighter gold for accents and highlights.
  • Define semantic design tokens for both themes and verify WCAG AA contrast for text and controls.
  • Logo direction: minimal wordmark with a subtle sun, arc, or manuscript motif; avoid quills and generic fantasy clichés.

12. Technical architecture direction

Application shape

  • Web application with desktop-like UX and PWA capabilities.
  • Offline-capable core; cloud services are optional and should not be prerequisites for editing.
  • Separate domain logic, editor model, storage adapters, indexing/search, sync, and AI provider integrations.
  • Use a migration-aware data model and versioned project schema.

Frontend and editor

Use TypeScript and a component architecture suited to a complex desktop-style interface. Vue 3 with <script setup lang="ts">, Pinia, and PrimeVue/Tailwind is a natural candidate. If evaluating alternatives, document the trade-offs before committing.

Evaluate ProseMirror/Tiptap and Lexical for the editor. Select based on structured content, custom nodes, Markdown round-tripping, transactions, undo/redo, embeds, annotations, accessibility, and offline behavior. The editor choice should be an explicit architecture decision record.

Core technical boundaries

  • Domain layer: workspace, universe, book, manuscript, worldbuilding, relationships, validation, and migrations.
  • Editor layer: structured document state, commands, Markdown conversion, embeds, and annotations.
  • Storage layer: local project files, browser fallback, media assets, and local indexes.
  • Link/index layer: stable-ID resolution, backlinks, search, and graph data.
  • Sync layer: provider adapters, manifests, revisions, encryption, and conflict resolution.
  • AI layer: provider adapters, scoped context assembly, privacy controls, and reviewable suggestions.

Reliability requirements

  • Autosave with clear saved/unsaved/error state and recovery from interrupted operations.
  • Deterministic undo/redo and predictable document transactions.
  • Schema migrations with backups or recoverable rollback strategy.
  • Automated unit, integration, and end-to-end tests for core workflows.
  • Accessible keyboard operation, semantic controls, and responsive layouts.

Codex implementation workflow

  • Start by inspecting the repository; do not assume it is empty or replace existing work.
  • Create and maintain AGENTS.md for repository-specific instructions and PLANS.md for milestones and decisions.
  • Work in small milestones with explicit acceptance criteria and tests.
  • After each milestone, update the plan, document decisions, run relevant checks, and report what changed and what remains.
  • Do not scaffold every future feature at once. Establish stable boundaries and implement vertical slices.

14. Cross-cutting quality and acceptance criteria

  • Offline: an existing local project can be opened, edited, searched, saved, and reopened without network access.
  • Ownership: project content is stored in portable, documented formats; no proprietary cloud account is required for core use.
  • Integrity: autosave, undo/redo, snapshots, and recovery prevent silent loss of manuscript content.
  • Relationships: stable IDs and links survive renames, moves, and book-to-book references inside a universe.
  • Transparency: sync status, conflicts, permission requirements, and AI data sharing are visible to the user.
  • Accessibility: core workflows are keyboard accessible and themes meet contrast requirements.
  • Extensibility: new entity types, storage providers, editor nodes, and AI providers can be added without rewriting unrelated layers.
  • Scope discipline: advanced maps, real-time collaboration, and provider-specific integrations remain later-stage work until core writing and data integrity are proven.
